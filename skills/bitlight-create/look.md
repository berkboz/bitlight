# Reading a frame sheet

`look.mjs` writes eight frames: **rest**, seven lamp positions around the plate,
then the lamp dead centre. Look at the light sheet and the dark sheet. For each
question, a "no" means go back to the figure file.

## The rest frame (top left) — it is the thumbnail
- Can you name the object from this frame alone, at a glance?
- Is the face that carries the idea lit? (Not a black silhouette — see rules · 07.)
- Is there a shadow on the plate that tells you where the light is?

## Across the eight frames
- Does the **reveal** happen? Find the frame where it is clearest. If you cannot,
  the concept is not working under this light — change the geometry, not the kernel.
- Does every frame still read as the object? A frame that turns to noise usually
  means detail below 2 cells; make features bigger.
- Any **stray dashes or dots** (paper lines where there is no edge)? Two parts
  sit with a small gap between them; join or overlap them.
- Any **shimmer bands** (rows of alternating dots across a flat face)? A face is
  almost exactly grazing the light at that frame — usually fine; if it covers the
  hero face, rotate the object a few degrees.
- In the **dark sheet**, does the object separate from the ground? If the object
  is mostly ink at rest, raise its albedo or move the rest lamp.

## Numbers from look.mjs
- `march` is a one-time cost when the figure mounts; `re-light` is per frame
  while the pointer moves. Under 400 ms and 16 ms respectively.
- `ink` is the share of non-ground dots per frame. Around 15–30% is typical.
  Under 8% everywhere: the object is too small or too dark to read.

## Write down what you saw
In the hand-over, name the frame where the reveal is clearest ("lamp 0.9,0.65:
the slot shadow crosses the plate") — it proves you looked.
